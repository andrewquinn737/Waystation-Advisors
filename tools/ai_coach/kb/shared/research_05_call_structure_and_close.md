# 05. Call structure, discovery, and the close

Scope: the flow of a cold call from opener to booked next step, plus callbacks and voicemail. Research items are labeled **Research base (lower weight than our own call data)**. Our own recordings and outcomes should replace these benchmarks as they accumulate.

## The goal of a cold call

A cold call is not a discovery call or a pitch. The goal is **one small next step**: a booked 15-minute intro (Waystation: with the buyer's representative; Working Signal: with a specialist who can scope the records). The farther the ask is from the owner's comfort, the less likely the yes.

Research base: the Black Swan Group article suggests treating a first call as a short conversation of about 5 to 10 minutes aimed at uncovering concerns and setting up another conversation, not pushing to close. Source: https://www.blackswanltd.com/the-edge/fixing-a-bad-cold-calling-script . Gong reports that in some top calls, next steps took about half the conversation, and recommends keeping the pitch short to leave room for them. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats

## The five-beat structure

1. **Opener (about 15 seconds).** See 01_openers.md.
2. **Reason and one-sentence pitch (about 20 seconds).** What it is in their language.
3. **Two or three discovery questions (about 60 seconds).** Learn enough to be relevant and to qualify.
4. **Bridge and ask (about 20 seconds).** Link what they said to the next step.
5. **Lock the next step and confirm (about 20 seconds).** Date, time, email, who.

## Talk ratio and monologues

Gong's published numbers are inconsistent, so use the pattern, not the number:
- Gong's cold call summary says successful cold calls have the rep talking more, about 55% of the time, with average prospect monologues of 3.5 seconds on successful calls vs 8 seconds on unsuccessful. Other Gong posts cite 46:54 or 42% talk for successful calls. Sources: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats , https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-calling-tips/?app=1 . The Gong page reports rep monologues of 53 seconds on successful calls vs 25 on unsuccessful.
- Gong also reports 77% more speaker switches per minute on successful calls.
- Gong notes that reps who talk 25 seconds or less are half as likely to book a meeting.

Practical reading: a cold call is a two-way rhythm with short owner replies, and the rep must carry the first 30 to 60 seconds without turning into a speech. Keep turns short. Ask a question about every 20 to 30 seconds. Do not aim to hit a ratio. Aim to avoid long monologues and silence.

## Discovery questions (cold-call sized)

Keep them light, curious, and non-interrogative. Pick two or three, not all.

**Working Signal**
- "Do you keep job records, drawings, or inspection and test logs on a computer or paper?"
- "How far back do they go?"
- "Is it in one place, or scattered across a few systems?"
- "Has anyone ever asked you for that kind of information, outside of a customer or auditor?"
- Qualifier: "Is there anything in there you absolutely would not share, like customer names or pricing?" (This shows respect and builds trust.)

**Waystation**
- "How long have you owned the business?"
- "Have you ever thought about what happens to the business when you step back?"
- "If a good buyer came along who would keep your people and your name, would you rather hear about it or not?"
- "Is there anyone else involved in the ownership?"

Use calibrated questions (Voss): "What matters most to you about who ends up with the business?" Source: https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-use-calibrated-questions-to-negotiate-strategically

Listen for: the first reason they give, any emotion (pride, fatigue, worry), and any yes-ish phrase ("I guess," "maybe someday"). Mirror the last few words to get more: "Maybe someday?"

## Bridge to the ask

Connect what they said to the next step:
"Sounds like you've got [ten years of job logs in the shop system]. That's exactly what [specialist] looks at. Would it make sense to spend 15 minutes on a quick call so they can tell you honestly whether it has any value?"

Research base: Gong finds an interest CTA ("Does it make sense for me to give you more detail?") outperforms a specific date-and-time ask on cold calls. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-objections . Once they say yes to the interest, then offer two specific times.

## Booking the next step

1. Offer two concrete options: "Tuesday at 7:30 or Thursday after 3?"
2. Confirm their email and best number. Repeat both back.
3. State what happens: "[Name] will call you at that time, it's 15 minutes, and there's nothing to prepare."
4. Send a calendar invite and a short email within 5 minutes.
5. Confirm the day before by text or email (no-shows are common).

Sandler's up-front contract applies to the call itself: set expectations at the start and give the owner permission to say no. Source: https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/up-front-contract

## Handling "call me back later"

- Get a specific day and time. "Would Thursday around 10 work?"
- Ask what they want to hear when you call back, so the next call starts warm.
- Log the promise in the CRM and call within the window. A missed callback is the fastest way to lose credibility.
- If they will not commit: "Should I try again in a month, or is it better to cross you off?"

## Voicemail

Research base: Gong's 300M-call data. Leaving a voicemail raised email reply from 2.73% to 5.87%. Leave at most two (three or more dropped reply to 2.2%). Voicemail 1: about 15 seconds of context. Voicemail 2: about 30 seconds with social proof. Callbacks are rare, so do not ask for one. Voicemails can lower later connect rates, so vary the number if possible. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/should-you-leave-voicemails-when-cold-calling-heres-what-the-data-says

Voicemail 1 (Working Signal): "Hi [Name], it's [Rep] with Working Signal. I'm reaching out to [Company] about records from your jobs that we buy for AI training. I'll send you a short email with the details. Thanks."

Voicemail 1 (Waystation): "Hi [Name], [Rep] with Waystation Advisors. I'm working with a buyer interested in [trade] companies in [region], and I'll send you a short note. Thanks."

Voicemail 2 (30 seconds): add one true, specific proof point approved in the brief (e.g., the buyer's criteria, or the type of records). Never invent social proof.

Follow with a short email within an hour referencing the call time and number.

## Call outcomes to log

Booked, callback date/time, warm lead (follow-up), not interested (reason), wrong person, gatekeeper blocked, no answer, voicemail left. Log the objection text and the rep's response. These logs are our best coaching data.

## Funnel benchmarks (vendor data, treat as ballpark)

- Belkins 2025: 9.9% of dials connect, 58% of connects became conversations, 4.6% of conversations booked a meeting (about 1 meeting per 370 dials). Meeting-booking calls averaged 3.7 minutes. Source: https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks
- Cognism: industry cold call success rate about 2.3% in 2025; their own about 6.7%. Source: https://www.cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-success-rates
- Gong: average rep 5.4% connect, top quartile 13.3%, about 19 dials per conversation for average reps vs 8 for top reps. Source: https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls/

Definitions of "success" differ across vendors, so compare only against our own data once we have it.

## Close checklist

- Did I state a small, specific next step?
- Did I confirm date, time, email, and phone?
- Did I tell them what to expect?
- Did I thank them and end warmly, even on a no?
