# 08. M&A owner outreach (Waystation buy-side)

Scope: calling owners of small private companies on behalf of a real acquirer, to get an intro call. This is the most sensitive call we make. Public research on M&A origination calls is thin, so most of this is general-sales evidence adapted, plus practitioner anecdote. All sourced items are **Research base (lower weight than our own call data)**.

Hard rules (from the Waystation outreach doctrine, apply to calls too):
- Never claim a buyer we do not have. Never say anything about the buyer that is not in the approved brief.
- Do not identify the buyer or the seller beyond what is approved.
- Do not promise a valuation, a multiple, or a price on a cold call.
- Always know who the rep is calling on behalf of and from which line before dialing.

## What we know about owners

- Many owners are not ready. The Exit Planning Institute's 2025 national report says 57% of Baby Boomer owners plan to sell within five years but are unprepared on formal valuation. Source: https://exit-planning-institute.org/hubfs/25SOOR-Generational.pdf . Another EPI-cited stat is that around half of exits are triggered by unplanned events such as health, disability, or divorce. Treat these as vendor-adjacent and cite carefully, not as facts to recite to owners.
- Owners are already contacted by buyers. A SearchFunder commenter quoted a seller: "we get contacted by buyers every month and ignore them," and said the seller responded to a letter plus email plus call. Source: https://searchfunder.com/post/best-methods-for-contacting-owners-who-are-not-actively-selling (anecdote).
- Searchers describe cold calling owners as relationship building, not sales, and say the goal is a meeting, not a pitch. Source: https://searchfunder.com/post/cold-calling-whats-worked-for-you
- A Yale paper on search fund seller selection warns some owners engage with no intention to sell, and sellers who invest effort signal readiness. Source: https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-04/On%20the%20Nature%20of%20Seller%20Selection%20in%20a%20Search%20Fund%20Project.pdf . Do not over-invest in a polite owner who keeps deferring.

## Sensitivity and tone

The owner's business is often their identity, their family's income, and their employees' jobs. A cold call about buying it can feel like a threat or an insult. So:

- **Respect first.** "You've built something real" is true and disarming. Only say it if you have a true reason (years in business, reputation from their site).
- **Lower the stakes.** The ask is a conversation, not a sale.
- **Slow and warm.** See 06_tonality_and_delivery.md.
- **No pressure, no urgency tricks.** Owners can smell it, and it hurts the buyer's reputation locally.
- **Address the fear.** The biggest worry is often what happens to employees and the name. Say what the buyer wants only if it is in the brief ("keep the team and the name"). Never invent it.

## Confidentiality

- On the first call, you are talking to an unverified listener. Do not say "buy your company" to a front-desk person. See 04_gatekeepers_and_reaching_owner.md.
- Tell the owner that the conversation is private and that you will not share anything without permission. Only promise what Waystation's process actually does.
- Do not name the buyer on a cold call unless approved. If asked, "The buyer prefers to stay private until we know there's a fit. I can tell you the kind of business they are and what they like about the space."
- Do not leave voicemails that reveal the sale idea. Use a neutral voicemail ("a business matter, I'll send a short note").

## Timing

- Call the owner directly, when they can talk. Early morning or late afternoon may reach an owner who is not on the floor. Test against our logs.
- Do not call at the end of a month with a payroll rush if we know the business's cycle.
- Time of year matters in seasonal trades (landscaping in summer, HVAC at peaks). Ask "Is this a bad week?" in a no-oriented way: "Would it be a bad week to have a 15-minute conversation, or a better one in a couple of weeks?"
- Follow up. Cognism reports about 8 attempts to reach a prospect on average (vendor data). Source: https://www.cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-success-rates . Many owners will not engage on the first touch, and a multi-touch approach (call, email, letter) is a common practitioner recommendation.

## Curiosity hooks

Research base: Josh Braun's poke-the-bear style uses neutral curiosity questions instead of claims. Source: https://www.11x.ai/guides/josh-braun-cold-outreach-method

Good hooks (true, specific, no promise):
- **The buyer's criteria.** "The buyer I'm working with is looking for [trade] companies in [region] that [criteria from the brief]. [Company] looks like a fit on paper."
- **Valuation curiosity.** "A lot of owners have never had anyone tell them what a business like theirs is worth to a buyer. Would you be curious to have a conversation, no obligation?" This does not state a number or a promise. Do not say "we can tell you your business is worth X."
- **Succession curiosity.** "Have you thought about what you'd want to happen to the business when you step back, even if that's years away?"
- **Local knowledge.** A true observation from their website, for example how long they have been in business or a specialty.
- **Optionality.** "You don't have to be selling. It just helps to know what options exist before you need them."

Avoid: "exit strategy," "maximize value," "opportunity," "unlock," "synergies," and anything that implies we know their price.

## Scripts

**Opener**
"Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Waystation Advisors. I'm calling out of the blue. We're working with a buyer who's looking at [trade] companies in [region], and [Company] looked like a fit. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why, and you tell me whether it's worth continuing?"

**After "okay"**
"Thanks. The buyer likes owner-run [trade] businesses that have been around a while and has said they'd want to keep the team and the name [only if in brief]. I don't know yet if it's a fit, and I'm not assuming you want to sell. Would it be crazy to have a 15-minute call with [name] to see if there's any overlap?"

**Discovery (one or two questions only)**
"How long have you been running it?" "Have you ever thought about what would happen if you wanted to step back?"

**Ask**
"Would Tuesday at 7:30 or Thursday after 3 work for a 15-minute intro call? If it's not a fit, we'll both know quickly."

## "Not for sale" handling

Do not argue. Validate, clarify the ask, lower the stakes, leave the door open.

- **"We're not for sale."**
"Completely understand, and I'm not assuming you are. Most owners we talk to aren't actively selling. The buyer just wanted to introduce themselves, in case it ever makes sense. Would it be okay if I stayed in touch once a year, or would you rather I not?"
- **"I'm not interested in selling, ever."**
"Fair enough, and thank you for being straight with me. Is that a firm no, or more 'not now'?" Listen. If firm: "Understood. I'll take you off our list. Thanks for your time." Log do-not-call if asked.
- **"What would you pay?"**
"I can't give a number on a first call, and I wouldn't want to guess. That's what the intro call is for. They can tell you how they think about value, without commitment."
- **"Who is the buyer?"**
"They prefer to stay private until we know there's a fit. I can tell you [approved descriptor]. Would a 15-minute call with us first be okay?"
- **"Are you a broker? I don't want to list."**
"We're not listing your business or putting it on a marketplace. [Only say what is true about Waystation's structure, confirm with Mason.] This is just an intro conversation."
- **"I'm too busy."**
"Understood. When is a quieter time? Would it be easier if I called on [day]?"
- **"Talk to my accountant / lawyer."**
"Happy to. Is that [Name]? I can also send you a one-page note you can pass along. What email should I use?" Note: research suggests seller advisors are a real channel. A SearchFunder post recommends CPAs, attorneys, and wealth managers as referral partners. Source: https://searchfunder.com/post/cold-calling-whats-worked-for-you

## After the call

- Send a short follow-up email within the hour. Thank them, restate the next step, and do not oversell.
- Log owner sentiment, reasons they gave, partner or advisor names, and any timing signals.
- If they say "not now," record the date they gave and follow up then.
- Respect any do-not-contact request immediately.

## What to avoid

- Claiming the buyer has closed deals, has funding, or is "ready to move fast" unless the brief says so.
- Talking about price or multiples.
- Implying we know the owner's age, health, or finances.
- Pressure ("the buyer is looking at others this week") unless it is true and approved.
- Calling a number you suspect is the owner's personal line without a reason to.
