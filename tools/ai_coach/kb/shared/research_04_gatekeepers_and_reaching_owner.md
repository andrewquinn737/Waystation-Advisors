# 04. Gatekeepers and reaching the owner

Scope: getting from the first person who answers to the owner, for small private companies ($1-20M manufacturers, trades, services). Items with sources are **Research base (lower weight than our own call data)** unless stated. Log every gatekeeper outcome in our own call data, because the research on this topic is thin.

## What the data says (and does not say)

- Gong's published research I could find has no dedicated gatekeeper study. Its relevant finding is about direct dials: top-quartile reps connect with 13.3% of prospects versus 5.4% for the average rep, and the top group prioritizes direct dials and marks bad numbers. Source: https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls/
- Belkins' 2025 study of 175,000+ dials: 13.4% of non-booking outcomes were gatekeepers and 13.5% wrong person or number. Source: https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks . So roughly one in four failed dials is a person-routing problem, not a pitch problem.
- Timing studies conflict. Mid-week mornings (about 8 to 11 local) and late afternoon (4 to 5) show up most often. Belkins found 3 to 4 PM and 6 PM had the highest positive-outcome rates, Wednesday the best day, Friday the worst. Sources: https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks and https://www.close.com/blog/best-days-times-to-cold-call . One practitioner claims owners arrive earlier than staff, so call them early. That is anecdotal. For trades and shops, owners are often on a job site or the floor during working hours. Test early morning (before 8), lunch, and after 4 against our own logs.
- A SearchFunder thread of searchers cold calling owners: one says "if you are talking to a gatekeeper, you have the wrong number," another recommends building a relationship with the gatekeeper. Both are anecdotal. Source: https://searchfunder.com/post/cold-calling-whats-worked-for-you

## Principles

1. **Be a normal human, not a trick.** Use your real name and company when asked. Blount's reader notes and Close both say to be transparent, never use fake schemes. Source: https://www.close.com/blog/get-past-gatekeeper . Deception (pretending to be a customer, claiming a prior conversation) burns trust and will cost us deals in tight local markets where owners talk.
2. **Treat the gatekeeper as a person with authority, not an obstacle.** In a 10-person shop, the person at the front desk is often the owner's spouse, sibling, or office manager and has real influence. Close advises not calling them "gatekeepers" to their face and preparing for them as you would the decision maker. Source above.
3. **Ask for help.** Close lists "Maybe you can help me..." and "I'm wondering who can help me with..." as effective framings. A request for help lowers defenses more than a request for access.
4. **Be brief and specific.** Say the owner's first and last name, say why in one clause, and stop.
5. **Do not pitch the gatekeeper.** Gatekeepers screen pitches. Give a reason that sounds like business, not a sale.

## Small-company specifics

Small private companies differ from enterprise accounts:
- The owner often picks up their own phone, or it forwards to their cell. Try the main line and the owner's direct or mobile line if we have a verified one.
- The person who answers might be a dispatcher, an office manager, the owner's family member, or a shop-floor employee. "Is [Owner] around?" works better than "Can I speak to the decision maker."
- Calls to a landline during work hours hit people who have a job to do. Keep it short.
- Check call-compliance requirements for the numbers and states we call (do-not-call lists, state rules). Mason's team owns this, do not guess.

## Ready scripts

**Gatekeeper asks "What's this regarding?" (Working Signal)**
"It's about some old job records and drawings for [Company]. [Owner] would know whether it's something they'd want to hear about. Is [he/she] around?"

**Gatekeeper asks "What's this regarding?" (Waystation)**
"It's a business matter for [Owner] about [Company]. It's not a vendor call. Is [he/she] around, or should I try [another time]?" Do not say "an acquisition" or "buying the business" to a front-desk person. Owners keep this confidential, and spreading it can hurt them. If pressed: "It's personal to [Owner], I'd rather let [him/her] decide who to tell."

**"He's not available / in a meeting."**
"No problem. When's the best time to reach [him/her] directly? I'm happy to call back." If they give a time, log and call within the window. Ask: "Does [he/she] have a cell I can try, or is the shop line best?" Most will say no, which is fine.

**"Send info to our general email."**
"Happy to. Who would see it? Is it better addressed to [Owner] by name?" Confirm the exact address. Then still try for a callback time.

**"We don't take sales calls."**
"Understood. This isn't a sales call, I'm not selling anything to [Company]. I'm calling about [records / a buyer]. Would it be better to leave [Owner] a message so [he/she] can decide?" Close suggests offering to leave "a 30-second voicemail" for the boss. Source: https://www.close.com/blog/get-past-gatekeeper

**"Who is calling?"**
"[Full name] with [Company]." Say the full name and company without hesitation. Hesitation is the biggest tell.

**"Is he expecting your call?"**
Honest answer: "No, it's a first call. I'm [Name] with [Company] and I'd like [Owner]'s opinion on [one clause]." Never say yes.

## Alternate routes to the owner

- **Another number.** Verified mobile or direct line from the data pipeline (apply our own verification rules).
- **Another time.** Early, lunch, or after hours if the owner is reachable at the shop.
- **Voicemail plus email.** Research base: Gong found voicemails raised email reply rates from 2.73% to 5.87%, with two voicemails the maximum (three or more dropped it to 2.2%). Voicemail 1: about 15 seconds of context. Voicemail 2: about 30 seconds with social proof. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/should-you-leave-voicemails-when-cold-calling-heres-what-the-data-says . Do not ask for a callback, point to the email.
- **A short email right after a no-contact call** referencing the time and number you dialed. A SearchFunder caller recommends this to prove you are real. Source: https://searchfunder.com/post/cold-calling-whats-worked-for-you
- **Multi-touch.** Letter plus email plus call signals more seriousness. A SearchFunder commenter reports a seller said "we get contacted by buyers every month and ignore them" until all three arrived. Source: https://searchfunder.com/post/best-methods-for-contacting-owners-who-are-not-actively-selling (anecdote).

## Persistence

Cognism reports about 8 call attempts on average to reach a prospect, and InsideSales is cited for at least 6. Sources: https://www.cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-success-rates and https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats . Those numbers are mostly vendor, not owner-specific. Our rule: try at different days and times across 2 to 3 weeks, mark bad numbers, then stop.

## Wrong person or wrong number

Verify within the first 10 seconds ("Is this [Company]? Am I speaking with [Owner]?"). If the owner has sold, retired, or died, say sorry and ask politely who runs it now. Log it. Do not pitch a person who is not the decision maker.

## Practice

Roleplay each gatekeeper script with a partner playing three types: friendly, suspicious, blunt. Score on: used full name and company, no pitch to the gatekeeper, asked for a callback time, stayed warm. See 07_practice_and_coaching.md.
