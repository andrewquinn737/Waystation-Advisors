# 01. Openers: the first three lines

Scope: what to say in the first 10 to 30 seconds when an owner picks up. Applies to both offers (Working Signal data purchase, Waystation buy-side M&A).

Labeling: anything marked **Research base (lower weight than our own call data)** comes from vendor studies, books, or practitioner write-ups. Replace it with our own call outcomes as soon as we have them. Scripts below use placeholders in [brackets]. Do not state any fact about a buyer, a price, or a track record that is not in the approved brief for that mandate.

## What the first seconds are for

The only job of the opener is to earn the next 30 seconds. It is not to explain the product. The owner is deciding two things: "is this a human I want to talk to" and "is this a waste of my time."

**Research base (lower weight than our own call data):**
- Gong's analysis of 300M+ cold calls found the opener matters a lot. Their 2024 rewrite compared four openers: "Did I catch you at a bad time?" scored 2.15%, "How's your day going?" 7.6%, a permission-based opener 11.18%, and "Have you heard our name tossed around?" 11.24%. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls . Caveat: Gong sells software and the page gives no sample size per opener. Treat the ranking as directional.
- Gong's older 2018 study (90,380 first-interaction calls) reported "How have you been?" at 10.01% success, 6.6x baseline, and "Did I catch you at a bad time?" at 0.9% (40% less likely to book). Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats . The two studies disagree on the exact "bad time" number (0.9% vs 2.15%) but agree it is among the worst. Note "how have you been" implies a relationship that does not exist, which owners of small shops may hear as fake. Test it before adopting it.
- Stating the reason for the call early is associated with 2.1x higher success in Gong's data. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-objections
- Nooks reviewed top reps and found 53% skipped the permission opener and went straight to context about similar customers, 41% asked permission but added context, about 6% asked permission with an odd, specific detail. Source: https://www.nooks.ai/blog-posts/how-the-top-1-of-reps-run-outbound-calls . Small sample, vendor-reported.

The takeaway that holds across sources: **lead with context (why you, why them, why now), say you are a cold caller out loud, then ask a small question.** Do not open with "how's your day" or "is this a bad time."

## The three-line structure

1. **Who + honest framing.** Name, company, and a quick "I know I'm calling out of the blue."
2. **Reason for the call in one sentence.** Specific to their kind of business.
3. **A small permission or curiosity question** that is easy to say yes or no to.

Keep all three under about 15 seconds when spoken.

## Permission-based vs pattern-interrupt

**Permission-based** (Jason Bay, Gong's "tailored permission opener"). You own the cold call, give context, then ask whether they will give you a moment.
- Research base (lower weight than our own call data): Bay teaches opening with a short intro and asking for a minute to explain the call, and stresses a nice tone because it is hard to hang up on someone who sounds friendly. Source: https://www.everand.com/podcast/684128313/Club-Playbook-Three-Steps-to-Cold-Calling-Success-ft-Jason-Bay-of-Outbound-Squad
- Gong's version: lead with context, own the cold call, get permission. Example from Gong's guidance: "If what I'm sharing isn't relevant, you can hang up on me. Sound fair?" Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-objections

**Pattern-interrupt / context-first** (Armand Farrokh and Nick Cegelski, Josh Braun). Skip the begging and lead with a fact that shows you are not random.
- Research base (lower weight than our own call data): the 30 Minutes to President's Club framework is described as context-first openers, then a problem proposition, then objection handling. Source: https://echai.ventures/gtm/r/the-ultimate-30mpc-cold-calling-framework--r-pip2-22 . The "heard the name tossed around" opener (11.24%) is their flagship, but it depends on peers knowing your brand. We do not have that, so use the context part, not the name part.
- Braun's phone opener: "Hi [Name], this is [Name]. I was hoping you could help me out for a moment," followed by a qualifying check ("am I in the right place, do you handle X?"). Source: https://www.11x.ai/guides/josh-braun-cold-outreach-method

**Voss's version.** Voss recommends no-oriented questions because a "no" gives the other person control and they relax. The Black Swan article suggests a casual, friendly "Have I caught you in the middle of something?" and, if they say yes, "Would it be a waste of time to schedule another call?" Source: https://www.blackswanltd.com/the-edge/fixing-a-bad-cold-calling-script . **Tension to watch:** Gong's data says "bad time?" performs poorly while Voss recommends a no-oriented question. The difference is wording and tone. "Did I catch you at a bad time?" is a closed exit door. "Is it crazy that I called?" or "Would it be ridiculous to take 30 seconds and tell you why I called?" is a no-oriented question that still invites a few seconds. Test both against our own outcomes.

## Which to use for our owners

Owners of $1-20M manufacturers and trades pick up on a cell or the shop line, often mid-task, and have no brand loyalty to us. Practical choice:
- Default: **permission + context**, delivered slowly and friendly.
- Use the **context-first** variant when we have a specific reason (their trade, their town, a fact from their website).
- Never fake familiarity ("how have you been?") unless testing it deliberately.

## Stating a new or unknown product in one sentence

Formula: **"[Who we are] [buy/do what] from [business like yours] for [reason]."**

Working Signal: "We pay shops like yours for old drawings, job logs and quality records so AI companies can train on how real work gets done. Nothing confidential, nothing that identifies your customers." (Confirm exact payment and scope language with Mason before using.)

Waystation: "We work with a real buyer who's looking to acquire a [trade] business in [region], and I'm calling to see if a conversation would even make sense."

## Ready openers

**Working Signal, permission + context**
"Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Working Signal. I know I'm calling out of the blue. We buy non-confidential operating records from [machine shops / HVAC companies], like job logs and drawings, to help AI companies learn how the work really gets done. Would it be okay if I took 30 seconds to explain why I called, and you can tell me if it's worth your time?"

**Working Signal, context-first with a specific hook**
"Hi [Name], [Rep] here. I saw [Company] has been doing [precision CNC work] since [year, from their site]. That kind of long record of real jobs is what we're looking for. We pay businesses for non-confidential records like that. Is that something you'd want to hear about for a minute?"

**Waystation, permission + context**
"Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Waystation Advisors. I'm calling out of the blue. We're working with a buyer who's looking at [trade] companies in [region], and [Company] came up as a good fit on paper. Can I take 30 seconds to say why, and then you tell me if it's worth continuing?"

**Waystation, no-oriented**
"Hi [Name], [Rep] with Waystation. Would it be crazy if I asked you one question about [Company]?" (Wait. Then.) "We have a buyer interested in [trade] businesses like yours. Are you open to hearing who they are and what they like about the space?"

## Tone and pace in the opener

Slow down more than feels natural, smile, and drop the pitch of your voice at the end of sentences. Do not sound like you are reading. Details in 06_tonality_and_delivery.md.

## Do not

- Do not open with "How's your day going?" (7.6% in Gong data, reads as telemarketer).
- Do not lead with a long company description.
- Do not claim the owner asked for the call, that you spoke before, or that a buyer exists unless the brief supports it.
