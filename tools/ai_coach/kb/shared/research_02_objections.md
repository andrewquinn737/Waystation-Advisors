# 02. Objections: frameworks and ready responses

Scope: handling pushback on cold calls to owners for Working Signal (data purchase) and Waystation (buy-side M&A). Scripts use [brackets] for facts the rep must confirm from the approved brief. Never invent a price, a buyer, a client count, or a legal assurance. If the owner asks something we cannot answer, say "I don't want to guess, let me get you the exact answer," and move to the next step.

Everything attributed to a named author or study below is **Research base (lower weight than our own call data)**.

## First: brush-off or real objection?

Reflex and brush-off lines happen in the first 10 seconds before the owner has processed anything. A real objection comes after they have heard something and have a concrete concern.

Research base (lower weight than our own call data): Jeb Blount's *Objections* work separates reflex reactions, brush-offs, real objections, and conditions. Reflex responses and brush-offs do not need a counter-argument, they need a calm turnaround and a small question. Summary: https://kartick.substack.com/p/summary-of-objections-by-jeb-blount (secondary summary; verify in the book).

Quick test:
- Said in the first few seconds, short, no detail ("not interested," "busy," "send an email") = brush-off. Acknowledge, give one reason, ask a tiny question.
- Said after they heard the reason, with a specific worry ("is this a scam," "what data," "our drawings are confidential") = real objection. Answer it directly and ask what would make it comfortable to continue.

Expect about half of the "no" answers to be brush-offs. Belkins' 2025 data on 175,000+ dials: 31% of non-booking outcomes were hard rejections, 19% warm signals (follow-up or callback), 13.4% gatekeepers, 13.5% wrong person or number. Source: https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks . Hard no's are the minority, so one calm turnaround is worth trying before you leave.

## Frameworks

**1. Validate, label, secondary ask (Gong).** Validate ("That's fair"), label the situation ("Sounds like you get a lot of calls"), then ask a question that keeps the call going. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-objections . Gong also reports calls where reps stop after one or two strong objections are typical of average reps, while top reps keep going. Source: https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls/

**2. Voss labels and calibrated questions.** A label names the feeling without arguing: "It sounds like you get pitched constantly." "It seems like you're protective of your records." A calibrated question starts with "how" or "what" and hands the problem back: "How would you want a company like us to handle that?" No-oriented questions ("Would it be a waste of time to...") make a "no" safe. Sources: https://www.blackswanltd.com/the-edge/fixing-a-bad-cold-calling-script and https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-use-calibrated-questions-to-negotiate-strategically . Note: the "23% lift" figure floating around for no-oriented questions has no traceable source. Do not quote it.

**3. LAER (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond).** Listen all the way through, acknowledge, ask one explore question to find the real concern, then respond. Widely used in sales training. Best for real objections, too slow for reflex brush-offs.

**4. Turnaround for reflex "not interested."** Research base: Sales Gravy's turnaround is to sound mildly apologetic, name a responsibility the person likely owns, and ask who to speak to. Source: https://salesgravy.com/the-best-turnaround-script-for-the-i-am-not-interested-objection/ . Anecdotal, no stats. For owners this works poorly because the owner IS the decision maker, so use the "ask a tiny question" version below.

**5. Sandler up-front contract.** Agree on what happens and give the owner explicit permission to say no ("If it's not a fit, just tell me and I'll let you go"). Source: https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/up-front-contract . Lowers pressure, which matters for a new product category.

## Ready responses

For each: acknowledge, one-line reason, small question. Keep every response under about 20 seconds.

**"Not interested."**
"Totally fair, and I'd guess you hear that kind of call all week. Quick question so I don't waste more of your time: have you ever been asked to sell or license your job records or drawings for AI training?" (Working Signal)
"Understood. Most owners say that before they know what it is. Would it be a waste of time if I told you who the buyer is in one sentence?" (Waystation)

**"I'm busy."**
"Got it, I'll be quick. Is it better if I call you at 7 tomorrow or Thursday afternoon?" Then confirm and log it. Offer two real times, not "when's a good time." If they reply "just tell me now," give the one-sentence version from 01_openers.md.

**"Send me an email."**
"Happy to. So I send the right thing, what's the one thing you'd want to know? Is it who's buying, or what we'd pay for?" Capture the best email and confirm. Research base: email follow-up after a call is common, and Gong found cold-calling lifted email reply rate to 3.44% vs 1.81% without. Source: https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls/ . Do not just accept the email as a polite exit: ask the one question first.

**"Who are you / how'd you get my number?"**
"Fair question. I'm [Rep] with [Working Signal / Waystation Advisors]. Your number's the one listed for [Company] publicly, [business listing / your website]. I'm calling because [one-sentence reason]." Do not say a source that is not true. Do not defend. Move on.

**"Is this a scam?"**
Label it: "That's a smart question to ask, and I'd be skeptical too." Then give proof they can check: "We're a real company at [website]. We don't ask for any money, passwords or payment information from you. If it ever turns into a deal, there's a written agreement and you decide what to share. Want me to send our info so you can check us out first?" Only state the parts that are true for the offer. Waystation: "You can look us up at [website], and I'd be happy to have [name] call you from an office line."

**"We're not selling." (M&A)**
"Completely understand, and I'm not asking you to sell. Most owners we talk to aren't. The buyer I represent likes businesses like yours and wanted a conversation, no commitment. Would a 15-minute intro be crazy?" See 08_ma_owner_outreach.md.

**"We don't have any data." (Working Signal)**
"Most owners say that, then realize they do. Do you keep job tickets, drawings, inspection or test logs, or maintenance records, on paper or a computer? That's the kind of thing we mean. Nothing special required."

**"That's confidential / that's our IP."**
Validate and label: "That makes sense. It sounds like protecting your customers' information is a priority." Then: "We're not asking for anything confidential. We're looking for things like [anonymized cycle times, general part types with customer names removed, equipment logs]. You decide what's shareable, and we'd put it in writing first." (Confirm exact scope and the agreement terms before promising.) Calibrated question: "What would need to be true for you to be comfortable sharing something like that?"

**"What does it pay?"**
Do not invent a number. "It depends on what you have and how much. That's what the first conversation sorts out. [Confirm approved range language with Mason.] Can I ask a couple of quick questions about what you keep records of, so [teammate] can give you a real number?"

**"Talk to my partner."**
"Of course. Is that [Name]? Would it help if I explained it to both of you together, maybe [day]? I'd rather they hear it directly than get it secondhand." Get the partner's name and a time.

**"Gatekeeper blocks."** See 04_gatekeepers_and_reaching_owner.md.

## After the response

Every response ends with a question. If the owner says no a second time, thank them, ask once if you can check back in [3-6 months], and leave. A polite exit has more value than a fight (see 05_call_structure_and_close.md for callback handling).

## Practice rule

Practice objections in order of frequency in our own call logs, not in the order of this list. Our logs beat any of the research above.
