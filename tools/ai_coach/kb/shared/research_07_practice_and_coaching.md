# 07. Practice and coaching

Scope: how interns improve fastest. Roleplay structure, scoring rubrics, progression from opener to objections, and call review. Evidence on practice is weak, mostly vendor claims, so rely on the structure and on our own score trends. Sourced items are **Research base (lower weight than our own call data)**.

## What the evidence says (and its limits)

- AI roleplay vendors claim faster ramp. ATD cites organizations reporting 40% faster ramp and 25% higher first-year quota attainment, with no named dataset. Source: https://www.td.org/insights/benefits-of-ai-roleplay-for-sales-enablement
- Exec.com argues the benefit comes from practice volume: its benchmark is 50 to 100 practice calls in the first two weeks vs a traditional 5 to 10. Source: https://www.exec.com/learn/sales-ramp-time-practice-driven . Vendor blog, no control group.
- A Siemens partner blog argues cold calling fails less from lack of knowledge than from lack of practice handling unpredictable responses. Source: https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/partners/ai-future-cold-call/
- I found no independent controlled study that shows roleplay improves cold-call results. So: treat roleplay as sensible practice, not proven science, and measure our own before-and-after.
- Gong's data suggests what separates top reps is not talent but habits: direct dials, marking bad numbers, targeting well, and not quitting after one or two strong objections. Source: https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls/ . Those are trainable.
- Nooks' top reps frequently personalized and pitched outcomes rather than features. Source: https://www.nooks.ai/blog-posts/how-the-top-1-of-reps-run-outbound-calls (small sample).

## Principles of effective practice

1. **Short, repeated, specific.** Practice one skill for 5 to 10 minutes. Opener only. Then objection only.
2. **Deliberate, not just repeated.** Each round has one target ("slow down the first sentence"), then feedback, then another round.
3. **Out loud, standing, recorded.** Silent rehearsal does not reveal pace or tone.
4. **Realistic prompt.** The "owner" should be gruff, busy, or skeptical, not a friendly pushover. Use real objections pulled from our own call logs.
5. **Immediate feedback.** Feedback within a minute while the memory is fresh.
6. **Review real calls.** The best material is the rep's own recording.

## Progression ladder

Move up only when the rep scores 4 of 5 or better on three consecutive rounds at the current level.

**Level 1: Opener (week 1).**
Memorize the opener for each offer. Drill until under 15 seconds, slow, warm, with a downward ending. Score: pace, tone, no "how's your day," says "out of the blue," reason in one sentence.

**Level 2: One-sentence product and the first question.**
Explain Working Signal or the Waystation buyer in one plain sentence without jargon. Ask one discovery question. Score: plain words, no confidential-sounding claims, question asked, silence after.

**Level 3: Top three objections.**
Not interested, busy, send me an email. Use acknowledge, one reason, small question. Score per rubric below.

**Level 4: Trust objections.**
"Is this a scam," "how'd you get my number," "this is confidential," "we don't have data," "we're not selling." These are the highest-risk for our offers, since the category is new.

**Level 5: Gatekeepers and voicemail.**
Scripts from 04 and 05. Include a blunt gatekeeper.

**Level 6: Full call.**
Opener to booked next step, with a random objection thrown in at any point. Include callbacks and a voicemail.

**Level 7: Live calls with review.**
Rep makes 20 live calls, reviews 3 recordings with a coach, picks one change for tomorrow.

## Scoring rubric (0 to 2 per line)

0 = missing, 1 = partial, 2 = solid. Total out of 20 for a full call.

| Skill | What 2 looks like |
|---|---|
| Opener | Under 15 seconds, says name/company, owns the cold call, gives a reason |
| Tone | Warm, slow first sentence, downward endings, no reading |
| Plain words | No jargon, one-sentence description of the offer |
| Honesty | No invented claims about buyers, prices, track record, or other clients |
| Question quality | Short, curious, one at a time, pauses after |
| Listening | Mirrors or labels what the owner said, does not talk over |
| Objection handling | Acknowledge, one-sentence reason, small question, no arguing |
| Risk reduction | States what we are not asking for, next step is small |
| Ask | Specific small next step, offers two options |
| Close | Confirms day, time, email, number, and what happens next |

Guide: 16+ ready for live calls. 12 to 15 keep drilling. Below 12 repeat the lower level. Adjust as our own data shows which lines correlate with booked calls.

## Roleplay formats

**Pair drill (10 minutes).** One rep, one partner as owner with a card describing a mood and objection. Switch roles. Partner scores the rubric.

**Rapid fire (5 minutes).** Coach throws an objection every 20 seconds. Rep responds in under 15 seconds. Builds speed and calm.

**Gong-style call review (15 minutes).** Play a real recording. Pause at the owner's first objection. Ask the rep what they would say, then play what they did. Compare.

**Mirror round.** Rep says the opener three ways (rushed, flat, warm). Learn how each sounds.

**AI roleplay (optional).** If we use an AI practice tool, give it our real owner personas and objections. Do not let it reward long answers. Verify it scores honesty and brevity.

## Owner persona cards (for roleplay)

- **The Busy Shop Owner.** On the floor, annoyed, short. "What do you want?"
- **The Skeptic.** "Is this a scam? How'd you get this number?"
- **The Protector.** "Our drawings are our IP. No way."
- **The Pragmatist.** "What does it pay? What's the catch?"
- **The Not-Selling Owner.** "I'm not selling, stop calling."
- **The Partner Pusher.** "Talk to my brother, he handles that."
- **The Friendly Talker.** Chats for ten minutes and never commits. Practice politely steering to the ask.
- **The Gatekeeper.** Office manager guarding the owner.

## Feedback format

Use a simple loop: one thing that worked (specific), one thing to change (specific, observable), one drill. Example: "Your opener was under 15 seconds and warm. Your last word went up like a question. Say it three times with a drop at the end."

## Coaching from call data

Our own data beats every external study. Track per rep per week: dials, connects, conversations, booked, objections heard, voicemails, email replies. Review the top 3 objections where reps lose the call. Review 3 recordings per rep per week. Share the best 30 seconds of each week's best call with the team.

Benchmarks (vendor, ballpark): Belkins 4.6% conversation-to-meeting, about 1 meeting per 370 dials (https://belkins.io/blog/cold-calling-benchmarks); Gong top quartile 13.3% connect and 16.7% set rate vs 5.4% and 4.6% for average reps (https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls/). Interns should not judge themselves against these in week one. Judge by rubric score and by trend.

## Mindset

Cold calls fail most of the time even for good reps. Reps who treat each no as feedback improve faster than those who personalize it. Reward good process (clean opener, honest answer, polite close), not only booked meetings. Reps who stop after one or two objections are typical of average performers per Gong, so teach one calm turnaround before leaving.
