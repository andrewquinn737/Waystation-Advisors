# 06. Tonality and delivery

Scope: how to sound, not what to say. Pace, pitch, upspeak, confidence, filler words, and the stats that exist. This is the area where public research is thinnest, so a lot below is practitioner advice. Everything sourced is **Research base (lower weight than our own call data)**. Record our own calls and score them against this file.

## What the measured data says

**Filler words do not predict results.**
Gong analyzed nearly half a million B2B call recordings and found that top, mid, and low performers use filler words at similar rates, and filler use showed no negative link to win rates, sales cycle length, or conversion. "So..." was 33% of all fillers. Reps and managers tend to notice fillers more than buyers do. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/filler-words-in-sales . HubSpot's separate analysis reportedly agreed. So do not spend coaching time eliminating "um." Spend it on content, structure, and pace.

**Pace and who leads.**
Gong reports top reps adjust their speaking speed toward the prospect by only about 1.7%, while prospects adjust about 13% toward the top rep, within the first few minutes. Average reps shift about 7% toward the prospect. Takeaway: the confident rep sets the tempo and the prospect follows. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/elements-of-effective-sales-conversations . A third-party blog attributes about 173 words per minute as a typical sales pace, with top performers holding about 176 under objections and struggling reps rising to about 188. I could not verify that on a Gong page, so treat it as unconfirmed. Use it only as a hint: do not speed up when challenged.

**Monologue length.**
In Gong's cold call data, successful calls have short prospect monologues (about 3.5 seconds average) and frequent speaker changes (77% more switches per minute). Rep talk spans are longer on successful calls, but they are interleaved with prospect reactions. Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats . Practical reading: keep thoughts to one or two sentences, then pause or ask.

**"We" language.**
Successful cold calls had about 35% higher "we" to "I" ratio and 55% higher "our" to "my." Source: https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats . This fits a collaborative tone. Use "we" naturally ("we could look at that together") but do not stuff it.

**Upspeak (rising intonation at the end of statements).**
I found no controlled study on upspeak in cold calls. Practitioner view only: statements that rise at the end sound like questions and signal low confidence, which owners hear as a sales hopeful asking permission to exist. Reserve rising tone for real questions.

## Voss tone guidance

Chris Voss describes three voices: the late-night FM DJ voice (calm, slow, downward inflection), the positive/playful voice (friendly, light), and the direct voice (use rarely). MasterClass reports using the DJ voice for roughly 10 to 20 percent of the time as a calming move, and the playful voice most of the time. Sources conflict on which should be the default, so we should test. Source: https://www.masterclass.com/articles/chris-voss-on-mastering-tone-and-inflection-in-negotiations

Our default for owners: **warm, relaxed, slightly slow, a smile in the voice.** When they get defensive ("is this a scam"), drop to a calm, lower, slower voice with downward endings. The Black Swan article also advises a friendly, accommodating tone and says a stiff or bored tone derails the call. Source: https://www.blackswanltd.com/the-edge/fixing-a-bad-cold-calling-script

Jason Bay's tone advice: a nice tone makes it hard to hang up. Source: https://www.everand.com/podcast/684128313/Club-Playbook-Three-Steps-to-Cold-Calling-Success-ft-Jason-Bay-of-Outbound-Squad

## Practical delivery rules

1. **Smile before you dial.** It changes the sound of your voice. Stand up if it helps energy.
2. **First sentence slow.** Say your name and company deliberately. Rushing signals telemarketer.
3. **Downward endings on statements.** "We buy records from shops like yours." Not "We buy records from shops like yours?"
4. **Pause after your question.** Count to three in your head. Silence feels long to you, short to them. Do not fill it.
5. **Sound like you are talking to one person.** Use their name once at the start and once at the close, not every sentence.
6. **Lower the pitch and slow down on objections.** The opposite of what nerves tell you to do.
7. **Do not read.** Know the opener by heart and keep notes on three bullets, not a paragraph. Reading sounds like reading. SearchFunder callers say scripts make you sound like a robot. Source: https://searchfunder.com/post/cold-calling-whats-worked-for-you
8. **Plain words.** Short sentences. No jargon. Say "records" not "data assets." Read it aloud: if you would not say it to the owner at their shop counter, rewrite it.
9. **Match energy without matching panic.** If the owner is rushed, shorten. If they are chatty, give a bit of space. Lead the pace, as Gong's data suggests.
10. **End on a warm tone even if they say no.** Owners remember how you left.

## Confidence without bravado

Confidence comes from knowing the first 20 seconds cold, having a small ask, and being ready for the top three objections. For a new category you cannot sound certain about things you do not know. Sound certain about your process and honest about the rest: "I don't want to guess on that, I'll get you the exact answer."

Never fake familiarity or urgency. A small-shop owner can spot a phony tone in two sentences.

## Self-review checklist for each recorded call

- Opener under 15 seconds?
- Did I say my name and company clearly and slowly?
- Did my statements end with a downward tone?
- How long was my longest monologue? (Aim under about 30 seconds in early turns.)
- Did I pause after questions?
- Did I speed up when the owner pushed back?
- Did I sound warm at the close?
- Did I use "we" and plain words?

## Common faults and fixes

| Fault | What the owner hears | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rushing the opener | Telemarketer | Slow the first sentence |
| Upspeak on statements | Unsure, asking permission | Drop pitch at the end |
| Monotone reading | Script | Know it by heart, use bullets |
| Over-apologizing | Weak | One acknowledgement, then a question |
| Speeding up on objections | Defensive | Pause, lower, slow |
| Long monologue | Salesperson | Ask a question every 20 to 30 seconds |
| Chasing "um" | N/A | Ignore, per Gong data |

## What to measure with our own tools

If we have call recordings and transcripts, measure per rep: talk share, longest monologue, words per minute, number of questions, pause length after questions, and outcome. Compare reps who book vs reps who do not. Our own correlations outrank the studies above, which mostly come from software sales.
