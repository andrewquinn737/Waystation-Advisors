# How the AI coach works (M&A outreach)

## The three steps on this tab

1. **Connect Claude or ChatGPT.** Copy your AI link into Claude (Settings, Connectors, Add custom connector) or ChatGPT.
   Then you can say "coach me" or "run a practice call" there too. The link is personal: don't share it.
2. **Your coach.** Every real call where you reach someone gets graded a few minutes after you hang up: an opener score,
   an objection score, what to keep and what to fix, and one line to try next time. After every 10 graded
   conversations the coach rewrites your advice at the top, based on those 10 calls.
3. **Practice calls.** You call an AI owner built from one of our real calls. It answers the way that owner did.

## How practice gets harder

- **Opening lines.** Until your first three lines land, the practice owner shuts you down fast, just like most real
  owners do. Pass three openers in a row to move on.
- **Objections.** The practice owner pushes back with the objections you handle worst on your real calls, until you
  handle them well.
- **Full calls.** Whole calls from hello to the ask.

It only gets easier when you get better. A practice owner that hangs up on you is telling you the opener didn't land.

## The opener that works best on our calls

"Hey, is this [Name]? My name is [your name], I'm calling with Waystation Advisors. We have a client who is actively
looking to acquire [business type] businesses in your area. He is an individual operator, not a fund. He wants to buy
and run the business himself. Have you ever thought about selling?"

- Name the buyer. Calls that say who the buyer is keep more owners talking.
- "Individual operator, not a fund" answers the private equity worry before they raise it.
- The last line just asks. It doesn't assume they want to sell.
- Leave out "how are you", "I was researching your company" and how many years they've been open. All three lost
  owners in our calls.
- Never say you're an intern, and never say how you got their number. If asked, you found the business through
  research.

## When they push back

- **"Not interested."** "Totally fair. Would it be okay if I checked back in a few months in case anything changes?"
  Then stop.
- **"Not selling for a few years."** "That makes sense. Our client does work on longer timelines. Would you be open to a
  quick call just to see what that could look like, even a few years out?"
- **"Who are you, how did you get my number?"** Your name, Waystation Advisors, a buy-side advisory firm, and you found
  the business through research. Say it confidently and move on.
- **Confidential.** The conversation stays between you, and an NDA is signed before anything moves forward.
- **"Send me an email."** Say yes, ask one qualifying question, confirm the address, send it within the hour.
- **"What would they pay?"** You can't quote a number yet; that's what the intro call is for. Go straight to the ask.

## The ask

One ask only: a 20 minute intro call with the buyer, no commitment. Don't hang up without one of three things: a
booked intro call, a specific callback time, or a confirmed email address.

## Getting the most out of it

- Talk out loud with the mic. Typing a perfect line isn't the same as saying it.
- Know the basics of the owner's industry before you dial (the big national players, what the work is).
- Read the "line that cost you most" after each practice call and say the fix out loud before the next one.
