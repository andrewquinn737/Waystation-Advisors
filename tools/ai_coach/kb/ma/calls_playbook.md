# Calls playbook (M&A outreach, Waystation buy-side)

## What changed this week

This is the first version of the playbook, built from 81 weighted conversations.

- Naming the buyer type in the opener keeps 62% of owners talking vs. 57% without it. It is the single strongest thing you can add.
- Saying "I got your number from an AI scraper" or "I'm just an intern" appears in the failed moments of our highest-weight calls. Both kill trust immediately.
- "Not interested" is the hardest objection: only 16% recovery. "Busy" is the easiest: 84% recovery. Know the difference before you dial.

---

## The opening three lines

**Jesse's version (use this word for word):**

"Hey, is this [Name]? My name is [your name], I'm calling with Waystation Advisors. We have a client who is actively looking to acquire [business type] businesses in your area. He is an individual operator, not a fund. He wants to buy and run the business himself. Have you ever thought about selling?"

**Why each line is there:**

Line 1 confirms you have the right person and gives your name and company. Owners who do not know who they are talking to hang up fast.

Line 2 names the buyer. This is the most important part. Calls that name the buyer or client type keep 62% of owners talking. Calls without it keep 57%. The "individual operator, not a fund" piece comes directly from Jesse's successful calls and kills the PE fear before it starts. From call 00c44b6f: "He's a searcher... he just barely received funding and he's actively looking to buy business and run them himself. It's not like a private equity fund."

Line 3 is a low-stakes open door. It does not claim the owner wants to sell. It just asks. That is why it works.

**What to leave out of the opener:**

Do not say "how are you" (zero measurable lift, owners find it fake). Do not say "I was researching your company" (keep-talking rate drops from 62% to 43%). Do not say "I noticed you have been in business for X years" (keep-talking drops from 61% to 34%). Do not say how you found their number. Never say "I'm just an intern."

**Variant A (owner seems rushed):**
"Hey [Name], quick call. I'm with Waystation Advisors. We have a buyer looking to acquire [business type] businesses in your area. He is an individual operator, not a PE fund. Would that ever be of interest to you, even down the road?"

**Variant B (owner already said they get these calls a lot):**
"I know you probably get a lot of these calls. This one is a little different. I represent one specific buyer, not a fund. He wants to run the business himself. Would you be open to a 20-minute call just to hear him out?"

## The first reaction and what to say next

**Curious (53% of calls):**
Owner says something like: "I mean, not that seriously, but if the offer was good enough..."
You say: "Totally. I am not asking you to commit to anything. My job is just to set up a short intro call between you and the buyer. You just get to see what an exit would look like. It is completely low pressure. Would that be worth 20 minutes?"

**Brush-off (39% of calls):**
Owner says: "Yeah, I get calls like this every week."
You say: "I bet you do. This one is a little different because it is one specific guy who wants to run the business, not flip it. Would you be open to a short call just to hear him out?"

**Busy (5% of calls):**
Owner says: "I'm at work right now."
You say: "No problem at all. When is a better time? I can call you back."
This objection has a 91% handle rate and 84% recovery. It is the easiest one. Just ask for a time and get off the phone.

**Guarded (3% of calls):**
Owner says: "It depends on who you are."
You say: "Fair enough. My name is [name], I am with Waystation Advisors. We are a buy-side advisory firm. We represent one buyer at a time. I am just trying to find out if there is a fit. If there is not, no problem at all."

## Asking for the next step

Your only ask is a short intro call. Use this line:

"My job is just to connect you with the buyer. It is a 20-minute intro call. No commitment, no pressure. Would you have time for that this week or next?"

If they say yes: get a specific day and time right now. Confirm their email address on the phone and send the invite within the hour. Tate did this in call 0e58dbe2 and booked on the spot.

If they say "call me back": pin down a specific time before you hang up. "What day and time works best? I will put it in my calendar right now." In call 5ca4c13d, the owner said "four o'clock would be great" and Nate locked it in.

If they say "send me an email first": say yes, ask one qualifying question, then send within the hour and follow up by phone in two days.

Do not leave any call without one of these three things: a scheduled intro call, a specific callback time, or a confirmed email address.

## What loses the call

**Saying you are an intern.** Call 2fc2e74a: Jesse said "I'm just an intern" and the owner disengaged. You work for Waystation Advisors. That is your identity on this call. Never say "just an intern."

**Telling the owner how you found their number.** Call 93d7e22e: Jesse said "I got it from an AI scraper" and trust dropped immediately. If asked, say you found the business through research.

**Using "researching your company" in the opener.** The data is clear. Openers with that phrase keep 43% of owners talking. Openers without it keep 62%. Leave it out every time.

**Mentioning how many years the business has been open.** Keep-talking rate drops from 61% to 34% when you bring this up in the opener. It signals you are reading from a script.

**Accepting the soft rejection without one follow-up question.** Call 785c6b1b: Ian heard "call me in five years" and moved on. The right move is: "What are you doing to prepare for that?" Stay in the conversation.

**Not knowing basic things about the owner's industry.** Call a717274c: Jesse had never heard of H&R Block. The owner lost confidence immediately. Before you call an accounting firm, know who the top national players are. Same for any industry.

## Owner types you will meet

**The Saturated Owner.** Gets three or four calls a week. Skeptical but still on the phone, which means there is some curiosity. Acknowledge it: "I bet you do get a lot of these." Then separate yourself: "This one is a little different. It is one person who wants to run the business, not a fund." Keep it short. (Calls 93d7e22e, 2fc2e74a, 01820989.)

**The Not-Yet Seller.** Has a timeline. Five years, six years, not now. They are already thinking about it. Do not push for a call this week. Ask what their preparation looks like, mention that the buyer works on longer timelines, and ask to stay in touch. The goal is a callback in six months, not a meeting tomorrow. (Calls a717274c, 785c6b1b.)

**The PE Skeptic.** First question is usually "Is this a hedge fund?" or "Is this private equity?" They have seen rollups before and do not want to be one. Fix it fast: "No. This is one person. He wants to run the business himself. It is not a fund." (Calls 00c44b6f, 01820989.)

**The Price-First Owner.** "At the right price, I'm always interested." Open to talking but wants to know the numbers make sense before giving you time. Do not quote a price. Say: "That is exactly what the intro call is for. The buyer will walk you through what he would pay based on your numbers." Then ask for the meeting. (Calls 2fc2e74a, 903a2b0f.)

**The Relationship Builder.** Takes a few minutes to warm up. Wants to know who you are and who the buyer is before committing to anything. Ask one genuine question about their business or how long they have been in the industry. Listen. Then connect it to the buyer's background. In call 01820989, Tate asked how long Daniel had been in the industry, listened, and then tied it to the buyer's profile. That is what got the intro call booked.
