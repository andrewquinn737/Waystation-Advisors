# Router: data sales (Working Signal)

The calls playbook above comes first. It is built from our own calls and outranks everything here. This router is
the short version of the general research, used only where the playbook is silent. Each line names the deeper file
(ai_kb_docs track "shared", slug shown) that the connector can open with read_kb_doc.

- Opener (research_01_openers): say who you are, then a pattern interrupt that owns the cold call ("weird request"),
  a true reason you picked them, the plain ask, and one easy question. Under 30 seconds. Giving a reason for the call
  beats asking "do you have a minute".
- New category (research_03_new_product_selling): owners have never been paid for their records. Lead with the
  problem the buyer has and a concrete example of the record, say what you are NOT asking for (no money from them, no
  confidential files, nothing with names), and offer a real way to check you are real.
- Brush off vs real objection (research_02_objections): a first "not interested" is usually a reflex. Acknowledge it,
  give one short reason, and ask a small question. Never argue, never explain more than two sentences.
- No-oriented questions (research_02_objections): "Would it be crazy to..." and "Is it a bad idea to..." get more
  honest answers than "would you like to...".
- Gatekeepers (research_04_gatekeepers_and_reaching_owner): be brief, ask for the owner by first name, give a
  one-line reason, and ask when they are usually in.
- Next step (research_05_call_structure_and_close): ask whether it makes sense to talk more before asking for a time.
  The ask here is a short call with the founder, Arieh. "Call me back later" gets a specific day and time.
- Delivery (research_06_tonality_and_delivery): slow down on the ask, drop your voice at the end of statements,
  pause after the question. Filler words do not hurt.
- Practice (research_07_practice_and_coaching): one skill at a time, opener first, then objections.
