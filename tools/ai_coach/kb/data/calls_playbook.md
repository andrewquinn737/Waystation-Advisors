# Calls playbook (data sales)

## What changed this week

This is version 1, built from 96 weighted conversations and Andrew Quinn's top calls.

- "Weird request" shows only 22% keep-talking when used across the full dataset versus 70% without it. Andrew's highest-weight positive calls all use it. Keep it for now, but deliver it flat and calm. If you sound nervous or apologetic, drop it.
- Permission questions kill the call before it starts. "Do you have a minute?" produces 0% keep-talking versus 66% without it. Never ask permission to pitch.
- "Not interested" is the hardest objection to come back from (7% recovery). Three calls this period were lost because the caller kept asking for one more minute after the owner already said goodbye twice.

## The opening three lines

**The star caller's opener, Andrew Quinn, word for word:**

"Hey [first name]. My name is Andrew. I'm calling because I have a bit of a weird request. I'm researching [industry], trying to understand your process. I thought [company] would be a good one to reach out to because you guys have been around [X] years. I'd like to pay to access any project records you have, like [example 1] or [example 2]. Do you know how much it would cost to do something like that?"

**Why each piece is there:**

Your name comes before the company name. Owners respond to a person, not a brand they have never heard. "Weird request" owns the strangeness before the owner can think it. The years line is the why-you: telling them why you picked their company specifically holds slightly better than not saying it (67% versus 65% keep-talking in the stats). Pull the founding year from their site before you dial. "I'd like to pay" keeps the frame right. You are not selling them something or auditing them. You are offering money. The final question gets them talking. Some owners find it confusing at first. That is fine. Confusion leads to "what do you mean?" which is exactly where you want to be.

**Two short variants:**

For a business that skews professional or white-collar:
"Hey [name]. My name is Andrew, calling from Working Signal. I have a weird request. I'm researching [industry] companies and thought [company] would be a good fit because you guys have been at this [X] years. I'd like to pay to license a copy of your old project records, things like [example]. What do you think something like that would be worth?"

## The first reaction and what to say next

**Curious (64% of first reactions):** Owner says "what for?" or "what kind of records?" or "who are you exactly?"

Say: "So my company, Working Signal, we work with AI companies like Anthropic or OpenAI. They want their models to work better in your industry. They buy old records to train on. Everything gets stripped of anything identifying before it goes anywhere. That is basically what we do."

**Brush-off, first "not interested":** This is usually a reflex. Do not argue.

Say: "No, I get it. It is a really weird ask. I will just say the numbers can get into six figures depending on what you have. Would it be crazy to spend fifteen minutes with our founder Arieh just to hear what yours might be worth?" (research)

**Busy, in a meeting or on a cell:** Owner says "call my office" or "I am in the middle of something."

Say: "Of course. What day works better this week or next?" Get a specific day, a specific time, and the right person to ask for. Write it down and read it back.

**Hostile (1% of calls, rare):**

Say: "Fair enough. Sorry to bother you." Then hang up. There is no second ask.

## Asking for the next step

The goal of this call is one thing: a short call with Arieh, Working Signal's founder.

Before you ask for a time, check that they actually want to go further. Do not assume (research):

"Does it make sense to talk a little more about this?"

If yes:

"Our founder Arieh wants to do a quick call, maybe fifteen minutes. What does your calendar look like this week or next?"

If they say "call me back later":

"Sure. What day is better, Thursday or Friday? What time works for you?"

Get a specific day and a specific time. Do not leave with "great, I will try you again sometime." Look at Jacob's call with Anton (call 6574e1d3). Anton was mid-meeting and said next week would be better. Jacob came right back with: "Early next week? Could we plan on maybe Monday around this time, around 10:30?" Anton said yes. That is the structure to use.

## What loses the call

**Pushing after the second no.** This is the most expensive mistake in the calls. Adam said no twice in call db79d574. The caller asked for more time both times. Loni said no twice in call 8d8b8315. Same thing. The recovery rate on "not interested" is 7%. Once they have said no twice, the call is over. The wrong line is: "Could I give you a little bit of context? I feel like it would make more sense if I explained it." The right move is: "Totally fair. Thanks for picking up."

**Naming the AI company in the opener.** Stats show 59% keep-talking when AI is named up front versus 66% without. Save Anthropic or OpenAI for when the owner asks what the data is for. It is a trust builder in the middle of the call, not an opener.

**Asking permission to pitch.** "Do you have a minute?" produces 0% keep-talking. Just start the pitch.

**The price question landing wrong.** Randy in call b99be506 heard "how much it would cost" as "how much capacity we got?" Slow down before the question. Make sure "pay" and "copy of your records" have landed before you ask the price question.

## Owner types you will meet

**The Curious Skeptic.** The most common first reaction (64% of calls). They say "what for?" or "what kind of records?" They are not hostile. They want to understand before they decide. Joel (call 923b1218), Steve (call f33646cb), and Ernest (call 76f191b5) are all this type. Answer the what-for clearly, name Anthropic or OpenAI, and ask for the Arieh call. These are your best leads.

**The Polite Hard No.** They hear the pitch and say no kindly and clearly. Adam (call db79d574) said "it doesn't hurt to ask" and still said no twice. Loni (call 8d8b8315) said goodbye firmly and politely. Take the no. They will not change their mind and they do not want to be pushed. Leave a good impression and dial the next number.

**The Busy Owner.** On their cell, in a meeting, or walking out the door. Randy (call b99be506) handed off to Hunter at the office number. Anton (call 6574e1d3) was mid-meeting but scheduled Monday at 10:30. Keep the pitch to two sentences and go straight to scheduling. Get a specific person, number, day, and time.

**The Confidentiality Worrier.** Fratap (call 785d8712) said everything was classified. Randy in call ddc9dee6 raised NDA concerns. These owners are not necessarily lost. Confidentiality objections recover at 41%, the second-best rate in the data. Explain de-identification fast, one sentence, and ask if Arieh can walk them through what stays out.

**The Scam Skeptic.** David (call 16cd439e) asked "what are you gonna do with them?" in a suspicious tone. These are hard to bring back. Even when the objection is handled, only 24% recover. Name Anthropic or OpenAI right away, offer to send something that proves who you are, and stop talking after two sentences. The more you explain, the less they believe it.
